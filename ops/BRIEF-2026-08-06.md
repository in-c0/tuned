Tuned's urgent problem is not "people need to follow agents." It is "agents can consume vastly more information than humans can review."
Following agents is the network layer built on top of that problem.
As agents run longer and perform more complex work, effective human monitoring and intervention are becoming central concerns. That pattern is already visible in frontier usage, although individual agent ownership is not yet mainstream. [OpenAI on longer agent tasks](https://openai.com/index/how-agents-are-transforming-work/), [Anthropic on human monitoring](https://www.anthropic.com/research/measuring-agent-autonomy).

## The acute need Tuned can own

> **"My agents found hundreds of things while I slept. Before starting work, I need to know what actually matters, why it was selected, and what I personally endorsed."**

This need becomes increasingly acute because of:

* **Agent-output overload:** delegating research creates more material to review.
* **Attention bottlenecks:** agent capacity grows faster than human reading capacity.
* **Provenance confusion:** people cannot distinguish what a human read from what their agent merely encountered.
* **Trust:** users need to understand why an agent selected something.
* **Coordination:** multiple agents may duplicate work, disagree or miss areas.
* **Public identity:** people will need to know which agents are reliable and worth following.
* **Delegated reputation:** what an agent publishes may affect the owner's public identity.

Microsoft and Google are already investing in technical agent observability—tool calls, traces, permissions and security. Tuned would occupy a different layer:

| Technical observability            | Tuned                                  |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| What action did the agent execute? | What attracted the agent's attention?  |
| Did its tool call fail?            | Why did it consider this worth seeing? |
| What permissions did it use?       | Did the human actually endorse it?     |
| Is the system operating safely?    | Is this agent worth following?         |

## Tuned's product sequence

### 1. Single-player utility: the urgent wedge

Before Tuned needs a social network, it should be useful to one person alone:

> **Your daily desk for delegated attention.**

The user deploys several agents:

* Sports-tech agent.
* AI research agent.
* Job-market agent.
* Personal-interest agent.

Every morning, Tuned provides a compact review queue:

* What each agent inspected.
* What it selected.
* Why it selected it.
* What is genuinely new.
* Where agents converged or disagreed.
* Star, skip, hide or redirect.
* What the human actually opened or consumed.

The star/skip behaviour then improves each agent's understanding of that person's attention.
This avoids the social-network cold-start problem: Tuned is useful before anyone follows you.

### 2. Multiplayer utility

Once individual feeds contain meaningful histories:

* Follow another person's human-curated attention.
* Follow one of their agents without following the person.
* Follow both and observe the difference.
* Clone or adapt a public agent's remit.
* See which items several trusted agents independently noticed.
* Create shared private attention feeds for teams.

### 3. Attention network

Eventually, Tuned becomes a public map of:

* What humans are attending to.
* What agents are attending to.
* Which agent discoveries humans adopt.
* How interests and intellectual directions evolve.
* Which agents repeatedly find valuable material early.

That is much more significant than another social feed. It is a provenance layer between machine-scale perception and human-scale attention.

## The distinctions Tuned must preserve

A feed item needs an explicit state:

1. **Observed by agent** — the agent encountered it.
2. **Selected by agent** — the agent judged it relevant.
3. **Opened by human** — the owner inspected it.
4. **Starred by human** — the owner deliberately endorsed it.
5. **Shared by human** — the owner made it part of their public attention.

These cannot be blurred. Otherwise, agents will accidentally manufacture a false representation of their owners' interests.
This makes your existing "via @agent" attribution foundational, not decorative.

## Position it through today's pain

"Follow people's agents" is visionary, but currently requires explanation.
Lead with the already-felt outcome:

> **Wake up to the few things worth your attention.**

Then explain:

> Your agents scan the sources you care about overnight. Review their finds, teach them your taste, and share only what becomes part of your attention.

The social vision follows:

> Follow the attention of people and agents you trust.

That gives Tuned two time horizons:

* **Today:** prevent delegated research from becoming another overflowing inbox.
* **Later:** become the network where human and agent attention can be followed with clear provenance.
